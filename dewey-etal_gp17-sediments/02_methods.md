## Methods

### Bulk elemental characterization 
Elemental concentrations were quantified with X-ray fluorescence (XRF, Rigaku Supermini 200 WDXRF) and CHNS analysis (Elementar) on dried and ground samples. The samples were prepared for analysis in an anaerobic chamber (95 % N$_2$, 5% H$_2$ atmosphere). 
### X-ray absorption spectroscopy 
#### Bulk Fe K-edge EXAFS 
Bulk Fe K-edge EXAFS were measured on sediments from stations 5, 15, and 27 at depths of 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 15 cm (below the seawater-sediment interface). All samples were handled under anaerobic atmosphere (95% N2 / 5% H2). Sediments were dried anaerobically, finely ground with an agate mortar and pestle, and loaded into Al sample holders sealed with Kapton tape. The Fe EXAFS measurements were performed at the Stanford Synchrotron Radiation Lightsource (SSRL) on beamline 7-3 in a liquid He cryostat (measurement temperature of 15 K). Incident X-ray energy was controlled with a liquid N2-cooled Si(220) double monochromator in the $\phi$ = 0° configuration. Higher-order harmonics were filtered from the incoming beam by detuning 30% at 10.0 keV. An Fe$^0$ foil was used for energy calibration at the Fe K edge. X-ray fluorescence was collected with a 30-element Ge detector array. The data were aligned, normalized, background-subtracted, and analyzed with Larch.
#### X-ray fluorescence microprobe
Sediments from 5 and 15 cm depths (below seawater-sediment interface) from station 27 were analyzed with X-ray fluorescence microprobe. Dried and ground samples were embedded in degassed epoxy (EpoTek 301, Epoxy Technology) in an anaerobic chamber (95% N2, 5% H2 atmosphere) and petrographic thin sections (30 mm) were prepared from the set epoxy-sediment mixtures (Spectrum Petrographics). Thin sections were stored anoxically. X-ray fluorescence microbe (XFM) analyses were performed at the National Synchrotron Light Source II on beamline 4-BM. Elemental maps of K, Ca, Ti, Mn, Fe, Ni, Cu, and Zn were collected at 10 x 10 mm resolution with a 2 mm2 beam and a dwell time of 1 s/point. Incident X-ray energy was tuned to 8 keV with a Si(111) double monochromator in the j = 0° position. An Fe0 foil was used for energy calibration at the Fe K edge (7.112 keV). The maps were processed and analyzed with Larch. Individual Fe K-edge XANES spectra were collected from Fe-rich particles detected in the elemental maps. Spectra were normalized and flattened in Larch using standard procedures. A total of 172 spectra were collected.
#### Multivariate analysis
Principal component analysis was performed on the flattened point XANES spectra between 7100-7180 eV with a 0.2 eV interpolation grid using Larch. The number of significant components was determined by manual evaluation of the scree plot and cumulative variance, as the indicator (IND) function returned an unreliable minimum at 152 components. Five components were retained, capturing approximately 97% of the spectral variance. The first two components contributed ~87% of the variance, with components 3-5 contributing additional variance associated with subtle spectral features.  

Spectra were grouped by K-means clustering in the five-dimensional principal component space to identify populations of particles with similar Fe speciation. The silhouette criterion favored k = 2, but evaluation of the Ward-linkage dendrogram and cluster centroid spectra indicated that k = 5 produced groups with coherent spectral features. Sub-clustering was performed on individual clusters where visual inspection of member spectra suggested remaining heterogeneity.
#### Linear combination fitting
The multivariate analysis identified five distinct populations of point spectra but does not reveal their mineral components. Linear combination fitting (LCF) was used to determine the mineral composition of each point spectrum and their corresponding cluster centroids. Because the 28-member reference library was small enough to test exhaustively (3,682 combinations), target transformation was not used to screen references before LCF.

Each individual grain spectrum was fit as a linear combination of mineral reference spectra using non-negative least squares (NNLS). All combinations of 1 to 3 references from the 28-member library were tested (3,682 combinations per spectrum), and the combination yielding the lowest R-factor was selected. Fit fractions were normalized to sum to 1. Average compositions were computed for each cluster, and spectra with R-factor > 0.05 were flagged as potentially containing phases not represented in the reference library.

Bulk Fe K-edge EXAFS spectra from sediment depth profiles were fit from 2-11.5 k$^{-1}$ using a reduced set of mineral references informed by the PCA and grain-level LCF results. The reference subset contained 10 minerals spanning the dominant spectral types identified in the point analysis: hornblende (Fe$^{II}$ silicate); augite (Fe$^{II}$ silicate); biotite (Fe$^{II}$ phyllosilicate); goethite (Fe$^{III}$  oxyhydroxide); ferrosmectite (Fe$^{III}$  phyllosilicate); nontronite (Fe$^{II/III}$ phyllosilicate); aged mackinawite (Fe sulfide); pyrite (Fe sulfide); ilmenite (Fe-Ti oxide); and vivianite (Fe$^{II}$ phosphate). All combinations of 1-4 references were tested per spectrum with NNLS. Individual mineral fractions were aggregated into geochemical classes for depth profile visualization: Fe$^{II/III}$  phyllosilicate (biotite, ferrosmectite, nontronite), Fe$^{II}$ silicate (augite, hornblende), and Fe-Ti oxide (ilmenite), with the remaining minor phases grouped together. 
### Mössbauer spectroscopy
$^{57}$Fe Mössbauer spectra were collected in transmission mode on sediments from 1, 5, and 15 cm depths at Station 27 (core 20892). Approximately 100 mg of dried sediment per sample was prepared in an anaerobic glovebox and sealed between two pieces of Kapton tape. Measurements were performed at the University of Delaware on an IAMS 4th Generation instrument using a constant-acceleration drive system with a $^{57}$Co source. Spectra were recorded at 295, 77, and 4.2 K using a closed-cycle He cryostat (Lake Shore CCS-800-205). The velocity scale was calibrated against an $\alpha$-Fe$^0$ foil at room temperature. Spectra were fit with the Recoil software using an extended Voigt-based fitting (xVBF) routine,$^{1,2}$ with the half-width at half-maximum (HWHM) fixed to the inner line broadening of the calibration foil (0.148 mm s$^{-1}$). Data are presented as relative absorption normalized between maximum absorbance and the average minimum absorption. Phase abundances are reported as a percentage of total spectral area.
